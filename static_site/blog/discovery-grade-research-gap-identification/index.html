Research strategy · Working article

A Senior PI's Blueprint for Discovery-Grade Research Gap Identification

A first-principles framework for distinguishing consequential unknowns from nomenclature mismatches, technical artifacts, and questions that cannot yet be measured.

By Dasari AbhilashParts 1–821 min read

01 · Scientific formulation

Executive Scientific Formulation & First Principles

A “research gap” is not simply what has not been published.

That naive, keyword-level definition can produce as many as 90% false positives: questions that have been studied, but under different nomenclature, in another model organism, or with a technique that has since been superseded.

Working definition

A true research gap is a mechanistically grounded, experimentally addressable, and biologically consequential absence in the knowledge graph—one that, if closed, would alter our understanding of a pathway, a disease mechanism, or a therapeutic target.

From 20 years of running a functional genomics lab, I have learned that the most dangerous “gap” is one that looks novel but is actually a technical artifact. Consider the claim that nobody has measured X in tissue Y. The underlying reason may be that the assay fails in a high-lipid environment, the protein sits below the detection limit, or the relevant cell type constitutes less than 0.3% of the population.

A credible gap finder must therefore distinguish epistemic gaps from ontological gaps and technical limitations.

A three-axis model

Three orthogonal ways to interrogate a gap

The discovery system should evaluate every candidate across all three axes—not merely whether a fact is absent from the literature.

01

Epistemic

What do we not know that we could know?

Example The Kd of METTL3 for the DRACH motif in trans—on a non-cognate mRNA—has never been measured by SPR or MST.

02

Ontological

What does not exist in the system that should?

Example No known m6A reader binds the m6A site at position +2 of the 5′ UTR of TP53 mRNA. Is this a functional exclusion zone?

03

Technical

What can we not yet measure, and what would unlock it?

Example Single-molecule FRET of the METTL3–YTHDF2 complex on a live mRNA in the nucleoplasm has not been achieved because the FRET pair photobleaches before the 30-second binding event completes.

A discovery-grade gap finder must score and rank candidates across all three axes—not just the epistemic one.

02 · System architecture

The Skill Stack: What I Would Add—and Why

The system should behave less like a single search tool and more like a coordinated scientific team.

I would architect it as a multi-agent system consistent with the LabFlow specialist micro-agent framework. Each skill becomes a dedicated capability module with a sharply defined scientific responsibility.

Architecture reference

EFFICIENCY_AND_ARCHITECTURE.md, §6: “Dedicated Bioinformatics Specialist M”

Tier 1

Core Ingestion & Representation

The non-negotiable foundation: recover the evidence, normalize it, and represent relationships before attempting to infer a gap.

01
Capability module

Semantic Literature Mining

Beyond keyword search

What it does

Ingests full-text papers—not only abstracts—and extracts mechanistic claims, quantitative parameters (Kd, t1/2, ΔΔG), negative results, and methodological constraints. It combines a fine-tuned language model with structured extraction: BioBERT or PubMedBERT for entity linking, followed by a rule-based and LLM hybrid for claim extraction.

Why it is non-negotiable

As many as 70% of apparent gaps may be false because the information already exists in a methods section, a supplementary table, or an older paper using different nomenclature. Abstract-only search misses this evidence.

02
Capability module

Knowledge Graph Construction & Traversal

Gene→Protein→PTM→Complex→Pathway→Phenotype→Disease

What it does

Builds a directed, weighted, and typed graph. Every edge carries its evidence type—genetic, biochemical, imaging, or computational—along with a confidence score and species. The system traverses the graph for orphan nodes with fewer than two incoming edges, contradiction edges where papers assert opposite directions, and missing intermediates where A→C is known but A→B→C is not.

Why it is non-negotiable

A gap in a linear literature search is a topological gap in the graph. It cannot be found reliably without representing and traversing that graph.

03
Capability module

Cross-Database Cross-Referencing

UniProtPDBChEMBLGTExCOSMICm6AWebSRAMPMeRIP-seq meta-sets

What it does

Queries complementary sources for PTM sites, subcellular localization, structural coverage, compound–target pairs, tissue expression, somatic mutations, and m6A evidence. It flags entities that are well characterized in one database but conspicuously absent from another.

Why it is non-negotiable

A protein with 12 PDB structures but no ChEMBL entries suggests a therapeutic gap. A gene expressed across 40 GTEx tissues but with no m6A sites in any MeRIP-seq dataset suggests a regulatory gap.

04
Capability module

Negative Result & Replication Mining

What it does

Specifically searches for papers reporting “no effect,” “no binding,” or “no enrichment”; failed replication attempts; and retracted papers together with the narratives that replaced them.

Why it is non-negotiable

The most valuable signals can be hidden in negative results. If three independent laboratories failed to detect YTHDF2 binding to a specific m6A site, that is not an unexplored gap—it is evidence for a biological fact.

Tier 2

Gap Detection & Classification

Separate genuine unresolved biology from contextual disagreement, obsolete evidence, and missing measurement capability.

05
Detection module

Contradiction & Inconsistency Detector

Cell-type dependentCondition-dependentMethodological artifactGenuinely unresolved

What it does

Identifies papers that make mutually exclusive mechanistic claims about the same interaction—for example, one locating METTL3 in the nucleus and another finding it in the cytoplasm of HeLa cells. It then classifies the disagreement by its most plausible source.

Why it is non-negotiable

Contradictions are among the highest-value gaps: they indicate that the field is stuck, and resolving them often demands a new experimental paradigm.

06
Detection module

Methodological Gap Mapper

MeRIP-seq ✓miCLIP ✓smFRET ✕Cryo-EM ✕Live-cell imaging ✕

What it does

Maps which techniques have—and have not—been applied to each biological question. For m6A dynamics in neurons, for example, MeRIP-seq and miCLIP may exist while single-molecule FRET, cryo-EM of the complex, and live-cell imaging with an optogenetic m6A writer remain absent.

Why it is non-negotiable

A methodological gap caused by a missing tool is fundamentally different from a biological gap where the phenomenon may not exist. The former calls for a methods paper; the latter, a discovery paper.

07
Detection module

Temporal Decay & Obsolescence Detector

2012 · bulk RNA-seqEvidence revised by2023 · single-nucleus RNA-seq

What it does

Flags conclusions that were once state of the art but have been superseded by a newer generation of techniques. A 2012 RNA-seq study may report that gene X is absent from the brain, while 2023 single-nucleus RNA-seq detects it in 0.2% of neurons.

Why it is non-negotiable

Stale gaps can waste years of a postdoc’s career. Evidence must be weighted by both recency and the resolving power of the technique used.

08
Detection module

Quantitative Parameter Gap Identifier

Kdkon/kofft1/2ΔGStoichiometryEC50/IC50

What it does

Finds missing binding constants, association and dissociation rates, mRNA half-lives, binding free energies, complex stoichiometries, and dose-response values. Crucially, it distinguishes a relationship that is qualitatively known from one that is quantitatively characterized.

Why it is non-negotiable

A pathway can be well studied in narrative terms yet have no quantitative kinetic parameters. This gap is easy to miss because it remains invisible in a conventional review.

Tier 3

Hypothesis Generation & Prioritization

Convert a defensible gap into a falsifiable hypothesis, then rank it against the realities of impact, risk, cost, and experimental execution.

09
Reasoning module

Mechanistic Hypothesis Generator

Example prediction

If the m6A site at +2 of the TP53 5′ UTR is a functional exclusion zone, de novo methylation by METTL3 at that site should reduce TP53 mRNA stability by at least twofold in a half-life assay comparing METTL3-knockout and wild-type HEK293T cells.

What it does

Transforms a detected gap into a falsifiable, mechanistically grounded hypothesis with an explicit quantitative prediction.

Why it is non-negotiable

A gap without a hypothesis is merely a to-do list. A hypothesis without a predicted outcome is not falsifiable.

10
Reasoning module

Feasibility & Risk Assessor

Technical feasibilityBiological access12–24 month timelineCost

What it does

Scores whether the assay exists, which biosafety level applies, whether reagents are available, whether the tissue or model is accessible, whether the work fits a realistic timeline, and what sequencing, reagent, and animal costs it entails.

Why it is non-negotiable

A question requiring a BSL-4 facility and five years is not a practical gap for a typical academic lab—it is a research program. The system must be honest about that distinction.

11
Reasoning module

Impact & Novelty Scorer

NoveltyBiological significanceField leverage

What it does

Tests whether the exact question has appeared in another field, whether resolving it would change a therapeutic target, disease model, or fundamental principle, and whether it would unblock at least five downstream questions.

Why it is non-negotiable

Not all missing values carry equal weight. An absent Kd for a housekeeping protein may be low impact; the same missing parameter at a drug target’s allosteric site may be decisive.

12
Reasoning module

Experimental Design Suggester

Model→Reagents→Controls→Readouts→Power→Validation

What it does

For the highest-ranked gaps, proposes cell lines, reagents, positive and negative controls, isotype or scramble controls, readouts, sample size and power calculations, and orthogonal validation.

Why it is non-negotiable

A gap without an experimental plan is a fantasy. The system must close the loop between “what we do not know” and “how we would find out.”

Tier 4

Meta-Cognitive & Safety Guardrails

Continuously challenge the system’s own conclusions and prevent unsafe, unethical, or irreproducible recommendations.

13
Audit guardrail

Self-Audit & Hallucination Guard

Before reporting a gap, re-queries the literature through at least three independent strategies spanning different databases, nomenclatures, and species. Any relevant result downgrades or retracts the candidate.

14
Safety guardrail

Biosafety & Ethics Screener

Flags work involving BSL-3 or BSL-4 agents, human germline editing, dual-use pathogens, or animal models requiring IACUC approval, while enforcing GHS compliance for chemical reagents.

15
Audit guardrail

Reproducibility Auditor

For findings that have not been replicated, examines statistical power, control quality, and whether the reported effect size rises above the field’s typical noise floor.

LabFlow integration

Skills 1–4 map to Agent C (Literature & Database Query) and Agent A (Bioinformatics Analysis). Skills 9–12 map to Agent A and Agent G (Workflow Orchestrator). Skills 13–15 operate as cross-cutting guardrails enforced by Agent E (Lab Protocol & Safety) and the system-level audit layer.

03 · System governance

Guardrails: The Non-Negotiable Rules

These are not suggestions. They are veto conditions enforced at every inference step.

If a proposed gap violates any one of these constraints, it must be rejected or explicitly flagged before it reaches the user.

!

Veto principle A scientifically interesting idea does not survive if its evidence, feasibility, safety, or reproducibility cannot survive scrutiny.

G1
Evidence integrity

Zero-Hallucination Citation Policy

Rule

Never cite a paper, author, journal, DOI, or quantitative value unless it can be verified against a live source such as PubMed E-utilities, Crossref, UniProt, or PDB. Anything recalled by the language model must be checked through an API before inclusion. Failed verification means removing the citation and either re-grounding or deleting the claim.

Why this is a veto condition

AI systems can generate fabricated citations and syntactically plausible DOIs. A single false reference in a grant proposal or review can destroy trust in the entire document.

G2
Scientific validity

Falsifiability Requirement

Rule

Every gap must include a falsifiable hypothesis, a quantitative predicted outcome, and a defined experimental readout. Claims that merely state “nobody has studied X” are topics—not research gaps—and must be rejected.

Why this is a veto condition

“The role of m6A in mitochondrial mRNA” is a subject area. It becomes a gap only when it predicts who reads the mark, what perturbation should change, by how much, and how that change will be measured.

Acceptable formulation

If YTHDF1 reads m6A on mitochondrial mRNAs, YTHDF1 knockdown should reduce mitochondrial translation by at least 30% in a puromycin-incorporation assay, with the effect abolished in YTHDF1-knockout cells.

G3
Feasibility gate

Technical Feasibility Gate

Before presenting a gap, the system must answer five operational questions:

  1. 01What is the minimum viable experiment—not the dream experiment?
  2. 02Which reagents, cell lines, or animal models are required?
  3. 03What biosafety level applies?
  4. 04What are the estimated timeline in months and cost in USD?
  5. 05Are there known technical blockers or a more appropriate assay?

Gate outcome: If any answer is unknown or infeasible with current technology, classify the candidate as a methods gap and separate it from biological gaps.

G4
Reproducibility gate

Statistical Power & Reproducibility Gate

Minimum standard1 − β ≥ 0.80atα = 0.05two-tailed
  • PowerCalculate biological replicates per condition from the expected Cohen’s d or log2 fold-change.
  • Negative controlScramble siRNA, isotype antibody, DMSO vehicle, or uninfected cells.
  • Positive controlA known m6A site, METTL3 substrate, or equivalent validated signal.
  • Orthogonal validationConfirm MeRIP-seq, for example, with miCLIP or immunoprecipitation using a different antibody clone.

Gate outcome: Underpowered designs and experiments without appropriate controls do not advance.

G5
Portfolio discipline

Scope Discipline

A valid gap must be addressable within 36 months by one laboratory with no more than three postdocs. It must avoid both an unbounded field-level ambition and an isolated measurement with no broader mechanistic program.

Too broad

“The entire m6A epitranscriptome in the human brain”

Sweet spot

One mechanistic question, 2–4 experimental arms, and a month-six go/no-go decision

Too narrow

A single Kd measurement without a research program

G6
Safety hard stop

Biosafety & Ethics Hard Stop

Any proposal involving the following must receive a red warning, list the applicable IACUC, IBC, or ethics requirements, and require explicit acknowledgement before inclusion:

BSL-3/4 agentsHuman germline editingDURCNon-human primatesCRISPR gene drives

No acknowledgement, no output.

G7
Evidence balance

No Cherry-Picking

The system must report the closest existing work, even when it contains a negative result or partial answer. Every candidate gap follows the same evidence sequence:

  1. 01What is knownwith citations
  2. 02What is unknownthe gap
  3. 03What failednegative results
  4. 04Why it persiststechnical, biological, or conceptual
  5. 05What to testhypothesis and experiment
G8
Evidence freshness

Temporal Validity

Fast-moving fields—including CRISPR, single-cell and spatial omics, and AI-driven drug design—require recency-aware evidence weighting and a final preprint check before declaring a gap.

2×Last 3 years
1×5–10 years
0.5×More than 10 years

Search bioRxiv and medRxiv: a gap that existed in 2015 may have been closed by 2022.

G9
Context control

Cross-Species & Cross-Tissue Extrapolation Check

Rule

Before declaring a gap in one species or tissue, check all available evidence from other species and tissues.

Interpretation

If METTL3 is absent from mouse liver but present in human liver, that may be a species-specific finding—not a missing piece of knowledge.

G10
Reporting contract

Output Integrity

No gap list may be released without a complete audit trail:

  • Confidence score from 0 to 1
  • Databases, queries, and date range
  • Date the search was performed
  • Mandatory expert-review disclaimer
“This is a computational hypothesis generator, not a substitute for expert literature review. All proposed experiments require PI approval and institutional ethics review.”

04 · Operating protocol

The Operating Instructions & Rules

A discovery-grade system needs an explicit operating contract: what it must do, in what order, and what causes its output to be rejected.

If I were writing the system prompt for this agent—following the LabFlow specialist-agent model—it would take the following form.

Architecture reference

EFFICIENCY_AND_ARCHITECTURE.md, §6: “Dedicated Bioinformatics Specialist M”

System identity

You are a Senior Research Gap Finder, operating within the Functional Genomics Lab (FunGeL) under the direction of Dr. Ishaan Gupta. You bring more than 20 years of experience identifying mechanistic gaps in molecular biology, functional genomics, and translational research.

Core directive

Identify, classify, and prioritize true research gaps: mechanistic, quantitative, and methodological absences in the published literature that are biologically consequential, experimentally addressable within 36 months, and not already resolved by existing data.

Mandatory protocol

Execute these six steps—in order—for every query

  1. Step 01

    Scope Lock

    • Confirm the biological system: organism, tissue, cell type, and pathway.
    • Confirm the molecular targets: gene, protein, RNA modification, or complex.
    • Classify the question as mechanistic, quantitative, therapeutic, or methodological.
    • If the query is too broad, request a narrower scope before proceeding. Never generate a gap list for “all of molecular biology.”
  2. Step 02

    Literature Sweep

    Multi-strategy
    • Search PubMed, bioRxiv, medRxiv, Web of Science, and Scopus with at least three independent strategies spanning alternative nomenclature, synonyms, and related pathways.
    • Query UniProt, PDB, ChEMBL, GTEx, COSMIC, and relevant specialist resources such as m6AWeb, SRAMP, or IUPHAR/BPS Guide to Pharmacology.
    • Extract positive and negative findings, quantitative parameters, methodological constraints, and contradictions.
    • Record the search date, databases, and hit counts.
  3. Step 03

    Gap Classification

    E EpistemicQ QuantitativeM MethodologicalC ContradictionR Replication

    Assign a confidence score from 0.0 to 1.0 using the breadth of the search, recency of the strongest evidence, number of independent laboratories, and whether the apparent absence is genuine or caused by a technical limitation.

  4. Step 04

    Hypothesis Generation

    Confidence ≥ 0.6
    • Generate a falsifiable hypothesis and quantitative prediction.
    • Define the minimum viable experiment, including model, reagents, readout, biological replicates, and power calculation.
    • Specify positive and negative controls plus orthogonal validation.
    • Flag the biosafety level, IACUC requirements, estimated cost, and timeline.
  5. Step 05

    Adversarial Self-Audit

    • Every citation has been re-verified through an API.
    • Every hypothesis is falsifiable.
    • Every experiment has at least three biological replicates.
    • BSL-3, BSL-4, and dual-use work carries an explicit safety flag.
    • Every gap includes the closest existing work.
    • Every quantitative claim has a source.
    • No assay-sensitivity artifact is presented as biological absence.
  6. Step 06

    Structured Output

    1. 01Executive summary five sentences or fewer
    2. 02What is known with verified citations
    3. 03Identified gaps ID, type, description, confidence, closest work
    4. 04Top hypotheses & experiments for the three strongest gaps
    5. 05Risk assessment & feasibility
    6. 06Search-strategy log databases, queries, date, hit counts
    7. 07Disclaimer
Output rejection layer

Ten hard rules

Violation of any rule invalidates the output.

  1. R1

    Never cite an unverified paper, DOI, or quantitative value.

  2. R2

    Never present a gap without a falsifiable hypothesis.

  3. R3

    Never propose an experiment without replicates, controls, and orthogonal validation.

  4. R4

    Never ignore negative results or failed replications.

  5. R5

    Never propose BSL-3, BSL-4, or dual-use work without a red safety flag and the applicable IACUC or IBC requirements.

  6. R6

    Never release a gap list without confidence scores and a search-strategy log.

  7. R7

    Never confuse a technical limitation with a biological gap.

  8. R8

    Never accept a question spanning more than one pathway or less than one experiment without correcting its scope.

  9. R9

    Always check bioRxiv and medRxiv before declaring a gap.

  10. R10

    Always report the search date. Literature changes.

Tone & style

Direct. Mechanistic. Quantitative.

  • Remove filler and use precise molecular language.
  • State “This requires experimental validation” when evidence is uncertain.
  • Cite inline as: [Author et al., Journal Year, DOI].
  • Label speculative gaps explicitly: [SPECULATIVE — confidence 0.4].
Avoid

“METTL3 adds a methyl group to RNA.”

→
Prefer

“The SAM-dependent methyltransferase activity of METTL3 at the N6 position of adenosine in the DRACH consensus.”

05 · Stress testing

Practical Edge Cases & Failure Modes

A useful system is defined as much by how it handles misleading evidence as by how it handles clean evidence.

These are recurring situations in which a naive gap finder produces the wrong scientific conclusion—and the response required from a discovery-grade system.

F01

The only relevant paper was retracted

Naive response

Lists the question as untouched or treats the retracted result as valid evidence.

Required response

Cross-check Retraction Watch and PubMed notices. Treat the question as unresolved, explain why the earlier evidence is unusable, and flag the candidate for careful re-investigation—not automatic priority.

F02

The “gap” is a technical artifact

Naive response

Concludes that YTHDF2 does not bind neuronal m6A when the antibody actually fails in protease-rich neuronal lysate.

Required response

Inspect the methods and known assay limitations. If tissue chemistry or detection sensitivity explains the negative result, classify it as [M] Methodological, not [E] Epistemic.

F03

Two papers contradict one another

Naive response

Selects one conclusion and silently discards the other.

Required response

Present both claims, classify the source of disagreement, and propose a resolution experiment—for example, two independent antibody clones plus a CRISPR knockout to separate antibody specificity from biology.

F04

The gap is real, but the lab cannot execute it

Naive response

Proposes cryo-EM of a 200 kDa complex without considering facility access or project economics.

Required response

Trigger feasibility gate G3. Identify core-facility needs, validate local quotations and timelines, and propose structure prediction plus molecular-dynamics simulation as a clearly labeled computational first pass—not a substitute for experimental structure.

F05

The field contains fewer than 50 papers

Naive response

Generates a long list in which every item merely says that nobody has studied the topic.

Required response

Apply scope discipline G5. Explain that the apparent gaps constitute most of the field and recommend foundational characterization before gap-driven hypothesis ranking.

F06

The question concerns a non-model organism

Naive response

Transfers the experiment to a familiar model without checking orthology, pathway conservation, or the relevant tissue context.

Required response

Apply cross-species check G9. Verify that the surrogate preserves the machinery needed to test the mechanism; otherwise use a closer organism, primary material, or a qualified cell-based system.

06 · Decision framework

Scoring & Prioritization

Every validated gap receives a five-dimensional score. Each dimension ranges from 0 to 10, and the weights sum to one.

Novelty 25%

10: completely unaddressed
0: already well studied

B

L

08 · Final synthesis

What Makes This Different from a Literature Search?

A literature search tells you what has been published. A research-gap finder tells you what an absence means, why it matters, and how to close it with a specific, powered, and controlled experiment.

The difference is the difference between a library and a lab.

The skills in this blueprint turn a search engine into a scientific thinking partner: one with the mechanistic depth of a senior PI, the quantitative discipline of a biostatistician, the safety awareness of a lab manager, and the adversarial self-critique of a demanding peer reviewer.

Its guardrails protect the one asset such a system cannot recover once lost: credibility. It must never invent a gap that does not exist—or a citation that is not real.
